# Phase 2 — Scoring the Opportunities

Scoring scale: 1–10, and **10 is always "most favorable"** for every criterion (so for "amount of custom art required" and "technical risk," 10 means *low* art need / *low* risk — the good end). Fifteen criteria per the brief, summed to a /150 total. This is a decision-support tool, not a precise science — the totals are directional.

Per the brief's instruction to *"strongly penalize clones with no meaningful differentiation,"* pure genre clones (garden-growing, pet-hatching, mining) are scored honestly below but **disqualified from the top-3 shortlist regardless of raw total**, because their discoverability and revenue scores are structurally capped by an incumbent that already owns the keyword/audience.

## Top 3 (clones excluded)

### 1. Aquarium/fish-collector idle-sim — 126/150
Highest score on almost every axis. Combines a *skill-based* acquisition action (short, satisfying, replayable) with an *idle* growth/economy layer (the retention engine behind every successful Roblox simulator) and a *social* showcase/trading layer. Procedural fish (simple part-built models, scaled and recolored per species/rarity) mean essentially zero custom art requirement for MVP. No combat, no PvP, no base-raiding — sidesteps the exploit categories that hurt the steal/PvP-tycoon genre.

### 2. Idle clicker empire — 96/150
The safest possible build (near-zero technical risk, fastest to prototype) but scores low on uniqueness, social/viral potential, and monetization ceiling. A bare clicker has no natural "show it to a friend" moment and no collection/rarity hook, both of which are what let simulators charge for cosmetics and boosts without feeling exploitative. Good fallback if the aquarium concept turned out technically infeasible — it did not.

### 3. Vending-machine placement tycoon — 94/150
Genuinely novel (low saturation), reasonably feasible, but lacks the emotional "collection" hook that drives simulator spending (nobody shows a friend their vending machine route the way they show off a rare shiny fish). Weaker natural virality and a shallower content-update path (new machine types run out of design space faster than new fish species/biomes do).

## Deeper look at the top 3

**Aquarium idle-sim:** Closest existing comparables are *Fisch* (pure skill-based fishing, no idle/tank layer — different audience moment, "I have 10 minutes to actively fish" vs. "I want to check on my tank") and *Grow a Garden* (proves the idle-growth-plus-trading loop works at massive scale, but for plants). No major Roblox game currently combines "quick active catch" + "idle tank growth, including offline" + "showcase/visit other players' tanks" as the *primary* loop. That gap is the opportunity.

**Idle clicker empire:** Technically trivial, but "click a button repeatedly" has been done to exhaustion on Roblox and mobile; would need an unusually strong theme/hook to escape the bottom of search results, and that hook is not obvious. Deprioritized.

**Vending-machine tycoon:** Interesting as a *future* project, but the placement/route-optimization gameplay is more spatial-puzzle than collection-hook, which historically monetizes worse on Roblox (compare: base-building tycoons monetize on speed/space, not on emotional attachment to what's inside).

## Final selection: Aquarium/fish-collector idle-sim

**Working title:** *Fish Tank Simulator* (see `08_PRESENTATION.md` for naming rationale and alternatives).

**Why this has the best risk/reward profile:**
- **Feasibility:** No custom art needed to launch (procedurally generated part-built fish — see `06_ARCHITECTURE.md`). No combat, no complex physics, no MMO-scale networking. A single developer can build and ship the MVP.
- **Retention without artificial waiting:** Offline/idle growth gives a genuine "come back and check on it" hook (proven at massive scale by Grow a Garden and every incremental game before it) without inventing fake timers — the fish are always doing something whether or not you're online.
- **Monetization is additive, not restrictive:** every paid item (auto-fisher, 2x growth, extra tank slots, cosmetic décor, rare bait) makes an already-fun loop faster or prettier. Nothing is paywalled behind making the free game miserable.
- **Differentiated positioning:** avoids directly competing with *Fisch* (skill-fishing) or *Grow a Garden* (plants) on their own keywords, while borrowing the mechanics that make both of them work.
- **Update cadence a solo dev can sustain:** new fish species are a recolor/rescale of existing procedural fish parts plus a new stat row in a data table — cheap content that keeps the game feeling alive (weekly/biweekly "new species" or "new biome" drops).

This is the concept carried forward into Phases 3–13.
